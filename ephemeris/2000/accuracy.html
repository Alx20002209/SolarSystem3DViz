Continuous motion: what we checked

Coverage: 1 January 2000 00:00 UTC to 1 January 2001 00:00 UTC. The UI stops at 31 December 23:59:59. All 35 selectable bodies are covered. No extrapolation outside this year.

Positions and velocities come from NASA/JPL Horizons. Cubic Hermite interpolation connects samples using their endpoint velocities. Moon coordinates are interpolated relative to their planet, then combined with that planet's interpolated position. Display scaling is applied afterwards.

1,260 separate JPL check times: 36 times per body, three interior fractions of one interval in each month. These points were not used as interpolation endpoints. The largest tested error is 249.592 metres for a body's source-relative position. The largest tested velocity error is 0.106742 m/s.

These are measured maxima at the tested times, not guaranteed error bounds for every second. They do not include uncertainty in JPL's underlying ephemerides. For a moon's heliocentric position, parent and Sun interpolation errors also contribute. Moon surface orientation and visual materials are illustrative. Compressed scale preserves directions but not physical separation ratios.
